## THE CORE DESIGN PHILOSOPHY

This site has one job: get someone to contact EDH Landscaping. Every design and copy decision serves that goal. Not brand awareness. Not education. Not showcasing every service. Contact.

The target customer is a homeowner in El Dorado Hills or Folsom who is tired of thinking about their yard. They are not shopping for features. They want it gone from their to-do list. The site must speak to that feeling, not to lawn care specs.

**What builds trust for this customer, in order of importance:**
1. Photos of real work — before/afters, not stock imagery (use placeholder divs with labels like [BEFORE/AFTER PHOTO: backyard, El Dorado Hills] so the client knows exactly what to shoot)
2. Real reviews from real neighbors — name + city, not just stars
3. The crew's face — a human being, not a logo (use [CREW PHOTO PLACEHOLDER])
4. A real phone number, visible always
5. Local specificity — mention El Dorado Hills, the heat, fescue grass, the hills, the foothills
6. Simplicity — nothing complicated to figure out

**What kills trust for this customer:**
- Stock photography of perfect lawns
- Jargon or service feature lists
- Too many choices
- Forms with more than 3 fields
- Anything that feels like a big company

## COPY RULES — ENFORCE THESE EVERYWHERE

1. Never say "cut" — say "visit", "service", or "we come out"
2. Never say "lawn care" generically — say "your yard" or be specific about what's happening
3. Never say "we provide quality service" or any variation — say what actually happens
4. Never use the word "seamless", "robust", "leverage", "solutions", "comprehensive", or "ensure"
5. Always use the homeowner's perspective — "your yard", "your schedule", "you don't need to be home"
6. Keep sentences short. No sentence on this page should be more than 20 words.
7. Phone number appears in: nav, hero, final CTA, footer. Minimum 4 times on the page.
8. Every section must have at least one way to contact — phone, text, or both.
9. Reviews use real names + cities. No anonymous testimonials.
10. Placeholder labels for missing photos must be specific and actionable (tell the client exactly what to photograph).

## ANIMATION & INTERACTION RULES

- Hero text fades in on load, staggered by line (150ms delay between lines)
- Trust bar items fade in on scroll
- Before/after sliders are draggable, with a clear visual handle
- Quote builder price animates (count-up) when sliders change
- FAQ accordion has smooth height transition (300ms ease)
- All hover states on buttons: slightly darker background, subtle scale(1.02)
- No parallax, no heavy scroll effects — keep it fast
- Page must load and be interactive in under 2 seconds on a mid-range Android phone on 4G

## RESPONSIVE BREAKPOINTS

- Mobile: < 640px — single column everything, large touch targets, phone/text buttons always visible
- Tablet: 640–1024px — some two-column layouts, nav visible
- Desktop: > 1024px — full three-column where specified, full nav

## PLACEHOLDER PHOTO STRATEGY

Everywhere a real photo is needed, render a styled div placeholder that:
- Has a dark green (#1a5c30 at 15% opacity) background
- Contains centered white text describing exactly what to photograph
- Has the same aspect ratio as the intended image
- Is clearly labeled so the client knows what to shoot

Example: a before/after slot renders as two divs side by side, left says "[BEFORE: Overgrown backyard, El Dorado Hills — shoot wide angle, show the full yard]", right says "[AFTER: Same yard after service — same angle as before shot]"

This way the site is fully functional and deployable immediately, and the client can swap in real photos when ready.
